## Cross-Corpus Findings

### F1 — The ritual demonstrably closes loops. That is the headline.

The operator asked what this analysis would recover; the first recovery is
that **the retrospective→discharge loop is already working wherever it has
run twice**. Regulated-development's first retrospective handed three items
forward (2026-08-10); all three were discharged the next day, run to
convergence, and verified by a recorded fix-pass review. Regulated-
engineering's first retrospective doubted its own backlog would move — its
second period reports items 6 and 7 closed within two days, in the sequence
the audit specified, and calls it "the strongest signal in the period."
The substrate's 08c exists because a dated chase fired and a session obeyed
it — the cadence mechanism's first live proof at that radius.

The condition, visible in every instance: **the loop closes where findings
are handed forward as committed things carrying dates or conditions** — a
carried-work thing, an armed trigger, a conflict with parties linked. Where a
finding stayed prose ("watch this", an undated recommendation), it recurred
(jmtm's June normalisation, still open in August; the submission-reference
vocabulary that got worse at exactly the moment June had named as the
trigger to act). The estate's rule generalises cleanly: *a finding without a
carrier is a hope; date the chase.*

### F2 — Capture outruns reckoning everywhere, until the first retrospective; then triage holds.

Identical arc in every first-generation retrospective: session-end harvesting
worked, disposition never happened. At first-retrospective time the backlogs
stood at 32 untriaged (development, six already untrue in their own files),
63 with 24 undispositioned (QMS), 14 all-active (overview), and the
substrate's own 109-of-126 active. Every second-period retrospective then
shows the discipline *holding*: development at 33/33 dispositioned with
honest reasons; QMS walking all 75 to 67-active/4-promoted/4-dismissed
without forcing merges; the career domain reckoning all seven at its first
pass. Two residues stay open as genuine judgement calls, not drift:
engineering's "does anything ever leave keep-active?" and the substrate's
declined uniform stamp (`a-uniform-answer-is-a-dead-judgment`). **Verdict
recovered: the disease was never capture and never the verdict — it was that
no reader existed until the ritual ran.** This is the same shape the
substrate found for dismissal conditions
(`a-stated-dismissal-condition-needs-a-reader`), now confirmed at estate
width.

and the record's quality is precisely what hides the gap**:

- regulated-engineering: `a-well-documented-stall-still-reads-as-progress`,
  then its mirror `a-well-documented-rule-still-reads-as-governing`
- regulated-development: the green surface over an unexamined premise (five

- jmtm-software: executes deadlines, defers structure — the well-formed debt
  with no due date loses to everything
- substrate: `tracking-artifacts-can-drift-from-reality`, recurring inside
  the attention layer itself (08c)

Per the estate's own rule that convergent independent invention names the

### F4 — A defined process with no run governs nothing.

Three corpora wrote their method down and then worked beside it:
code-architect's delivery process at zero runs in 57 days ("documentation,
not process"); regulated-engineering's lifecycle mandated as primary process
with zero workflow-runs and its three approval fields at zero uses; regulated-
overview's weekly estate agenda generator at zero runs while carrying the
heartbeat insight that condemns exactly that. Regulated-development holds
zero definitions and measured the cost (v3.36.0's entire workflow release
landed as a no-op there); jmtm-software found four insights parked with
"retires when the workflow skill encodes this" and no artifact to retire
into. The **counter-example is the substrate itself**: three floor sprints
and a seams run executed as pinned workflow-runs, independently verified —
instancing works where something forces the first run.

Cross-domain verdict material: the systemic answer is
`closed-loop-operating-state` Phase 2 (the dispatcher schedules invocations),
and the cheap floor answer is a one-line sensor — *a workflow-definition
past `draft` with zero runs pointing at it is an Info finding*. Both queued
below.

### F7 — The upward signal still has no path. This read is the proof, and the prototype.

Finding 6's first defect is the sharpest single recovery of this analysis:

### F8 — Discharge splits on the second person; the undated human wait persists.

Regulated-engineering sharpened it best: discharge is fast where it is one
person's act (everything agent-closable or CTO-closable closed within
days) and absent where it needs a second person — the account-access
blocker at 20+ days across a carve-out granted specifically to keep it
moving; regulated-development's standing-delegation conflict visible and
unresolved for three weeks *because* it needs the second operator (the
mechanism working, as that domain correctly notes); the park determination
three domains are sequencing against. The estate's own rule — date the
chase on any human-gated wait — is applied unevenly, and several of these
waits carry no date at all. This is `closed-loop-operating-state` Phase 3's
seat protocol arriving from the evidence side: second-person waits need the
same queue-and-cadence treatment as operator seats, or they become the
estate's slowest axis by default.

### F9 — Cadence: volume beats time. And the substrate's velocity taxes the estate.

The cadence question is answered by the corpus itself. Thirty-four quiet
days produced regulated-engineering's first backlog; sixteen busy days
produced a larger one ("the trigger should be volume, not time" — with
weekly commit counts of 8 · 22 · 9 · 26 as evidence). Regulated-development
ruled 45 days or engagement-close, whichever first. Code-architect's 69-day
gap is the negative case: long enough for an unstated objective to consume
the period invisibly. The substrate's own volume triggers exist in
`retrospective.md` but were never mechanically computed — the same
wallpaper-sensor class 08a named. Doctrine candidate queued: the floor
already computes the 60-day Info; add the volume line (insights and
conflicts since last retrospective are countable from the graph).

The second half is the honest cost the wave surfaced about the *substrate*:
eleven releases in 23 days meant regulated-development spent 19 of 21
commits on machinery ("kept ready rather than worked"), and code-architect's
whole period was consumed by absorption that its own instruments could not
see because it was never framed as work. Framework velocity has a
downstream absorption tax that domain instruments do not measure. Routed as
a finding into the substrate's next retrospective and the operating-model
sealing (Phase 5): a release-cadence honesty clause belongs in the doctrine.

### F10 — The floor held everywhere. The central bet is confirmed at estate width.

Uniform across all twenty documents, and worth stating because nothing else

strongest claim is no longer the substrate's self-report; it is twelve
domain retrospectives saying the same thing from the outside.
