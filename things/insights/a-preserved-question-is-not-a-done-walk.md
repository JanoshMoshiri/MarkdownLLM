# A Preserved Question Is Not a Done Walk

A step that nobody does can be given a backstop that files the question again,
and then another, and each one makes the question harder to lose while leaving
the step exactly as undone as before. The change-reconciliation pass had five:
the commit-time `candidates` advisory, the `type: cue` thing, the mechanical
`cues --raise`, the session-start digest line, and the retrospective's scan 4.
Read on 2026-10-05 at 3.46.0, the estate showed what they had produced. In the
framework root, 125 cue files, every one answered, 95 of them "not an
inflection", 83 by citation. In the domains, roughly 1,250 commits over six
weeks, zero inflection declarations, one cue raised in one domain and nine in
another that nobody answered in fifteen days; 184 and 146 unraised subjects in
the two busiest. The drift that unwalked change produced surfaced through
sweeps and accidents — a 44-commit contradiction, a 59-file repair, five stale
restatements found by a cold read.

The question was never lost. It was never *acted on*, because the act — the
walk — had been assigned to a declaration the operator does not make in
practice ("you don't announce an inflection point; you just say, we need to do
this now"), and every backstop inherited that assignment. A backstop that
re-asks is a memory aid for an actor who does not exist.

The tell is a count that only ever grows: a question preserved well and
answered never. When a mechanism's success metric is "nothing was forgotten"
and the work it protects has not moved, the mechanism is preserving the
question, not doing the work. The fix is never a sixth backstop; it is an actor
with standing authority to do the step on the signal the floor already gives —
here, the agent walking on detection, within bounds, with the human on the
residue.
