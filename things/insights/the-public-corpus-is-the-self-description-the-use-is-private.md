# The Public Corpus Is The Self-Description; The Use Is Private

## The Insight

Nearly six hundred things in the framework's public corpus are *about* the
substrate — specifications, plans, reviews, insights, cues. The work that
would show what the substrate *is* — repeatable workflows run from skills,
peer-workspace exchange through exposed faces, a human acceptance gate, an
intake-to-assessment-to-publication loop — lives in private workspaces no
reviewer can open. A cold reader therefore meets a system describing itself
and classifies it by the description: a knowledge or memory system. Two
reviews in five days did exactly that, under different briefs, and both had
to be walked to the use by the operator.

So the discoverability question the holistic review leaves open is probably
not answered by a better brief or a discovery workflow. Condition A of its
cold test — a broad identification brief against the existing entry route —
tests the public face, and the public face is the self-description. A fresh
reviewer who identifies the system correctly from it will have inferred the
use from claims about it; one who does not has read what is there.

**The implication:** the honest fix is a public worked workspace — not
`examples/`, which are demonstrations, but a workspace that actually runs a
repeatable workflow, exchanges with a peer and carries a human gate in the
open. Until one exists, every cold review is a review of the description,
and its classification is evidence about the description, not the system.

## Confidence

Medium. Two reviews are the evidence; a condition-A run against the current
public face would raise or lower it — if a fresh reviewer identifies the
workflow and exchange layers from the public corpus alone, this insight is
wrong about the cause.
