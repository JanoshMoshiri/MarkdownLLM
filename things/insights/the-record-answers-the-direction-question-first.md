# The record answers the direction question first

## The observation

The operator asked whether to keep building a desktop application or stay
on the substrate. Another agent, asked the same question elsewhere, had
answered with the generic line — protect your moat, don't compete with
vendors — and the operator brought it here unsure.

The answer was already in the corpus, in three places that needed no
opinion to read. The session-start digest's weekly buckets (72 · 106 · 87 ·
21) looked like a collapse and were, on reading the log, a displacement:
every commit for six days was product work, and the three highest-priority
substrate plans had not moved in five to six weeks. The direction
decision's own "current increment boundary" had grown into a repair list —
0.1.2, 0.1.3, 0.1.6 — with no mechanism that made it converge. And the
three triggers that fired that morning all pointed at the same place: the
human seat, waiting.

Reasoning added the diagnosis and the recommendation. It did not supply the
evidence; the record did, and any agent reading the same corpus at orient
would have landed near the same answer. The agent without the record
supplied a cliché.

The operator's response: *this is the exact reason I made it — I asked the
question and you already knew the answer based on what happened.*

## Why this is structural

The framework's thesis is that the accumulated, git-versioned record is the
memory no vendor owns. A direction question is the sharpest test of that
thesis, because direction is where advisors reach for priors. If the corpus
is doing its job, the priors are unnecessary: velocity says what moved, the
decision records say what was intended, the fired triggers say what is
waiting, and the gap between them is the answer.

This is also the moat made visible. What the operator could not get from an
advisor without the record was not intelligence — it was *this domain's own
evidence*, held in a form a reasoning system can read at session start.

## The rule

> Before offering a view on direction, read what the corpus already holds —
> orient's telemetry, the decisions the question touches, the triggers
> waiting on the seat — and say plainly when the record answered rather
> than judgement. The judgement is worth having; it is the second step, not
> the first.

Corollary: an advisor's answer that could have been given without the
record is the tell that the record was not read.
