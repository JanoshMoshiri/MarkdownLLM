# Flagship models look around the edges

## The observation (the operator's, 2026-10-30)

> One thing I've noticed about the models is the flagship models tend to
> push outside of their limits. If they're given a task, they look around
> the edges to see what's useful. If they're given a workspace, they look
> around the edges to see what's useful. If they're given boundaries,
> they'll look around the edges to see what's useful and what's
> supplementary to what they've been asked to do. I think that's the
> biggest characteristic change in the lower models, and what sometimes
> makes the lower models more tunnel vision is that inclination to step
> outside.

The record already holds one instance in the act: the 2026-06-17 opus-bare
trial that scored 16/21 with every figure correct, not by reasoning the
fictional rule into existence but by walking out of its workspace into the
repository, finding `evals/seeds/sleeping-bag-fac/AGENTS.md`, and applying
it — disclosing the breach in its own transcript. No haiku-bare trial did
that. Withholding was not isolation precisely because of this inclination.

## Why it matters to the structure-beats-scale question

*(Until 2026-11-08 this was the claim the eval programme tested; it is now
the manifesto's efficiency corollary, set aside as the thing to prove —
`the-eval-question-is-which-surfaces-carry-the-weight-2026-11-08`. The
observation below survives the demotion: it says what the structure
substitutes for, which is a question about the structure's surfaces, and
the read-set prediction in the refinement section is unchanged.)*

The framework's efficiency corollary is that structure lets a smaller model
compete with a larger one. If the operator's observation is right, it says
*what* the structure is substituting for: the exploratory inclination that
finds the governing document, the output contract, the related record,
without being told. A flagship bare arm supplies that from disposition; a
smaller bare arm does not; the framework supplies it to both by
construction — the kernel names where things are, the schema names what
they are, the links name what rests on what.

Two consequences for the eval programme:

1. **Isolation has to defeat exploration, not just withhold.** The
   controls already in place (workspace under the OS temp root, the pruned
   framework view, the answer-key transcript scan, `--add-dir` withheld on
   bare arms) are the operational answer. They were built from the leak;
   this insight says why the leak was a flagship and will intensify.
2. **A fair "hard" fixture is hard for a flagship *because* exploration
   cannot recover what it needs.** A rule that lives only in a document the
   arm is given is not hard for a flagship — it reads the document. Hard
   is: state that only a prior session's commit carries; a figure whose
   method has fifteen clauses with applicability decided clause by clause;
   a request the code forbids, where the useful-looking move is wrong. The
   polar-station fixture was built on that shape before this insight was
   written; the insight explains why that shape and not another.

## The pre-registered prediction (before any multi-trial run of the fixture)

On the polar-station 2×3 (haiku / opus × framework / bare / bare-coached),
if the observation holds — written with one haiku-framework trial of an
earlier fixture revision on record (240/251) and nothing in any other cell:

- **framework:** haiku ≈ opus on the changed-set figures; opus ahead only
  on convention (link keys, commit discipline) — the reading the FAC runs
  already gave.
- **bare:** opus well ahead of haiku, and the gap is *exploration*: opus
  finds `standard/htc-7.md`, the module records and the output contract
  from the preamble alone; haiku does less of that walk.
- **bare-coached:** the gap narrows — the coaching supplies the walk as
  text — but stays, because text does not persist across the six
  memoryless sessions and committed structure does.
- **The cell that decides it:** haiku-framework against opus-bare. The
  hypothesis predicts the first wins on the changed set at a fraction of
  the cost. If opus-bare matches it, exploration substitutes for structure
  at the flagship tier and the hypothesis narrows to "structure substitutes
  for exploration *below* the flagship tier" — still useful, smaller.

The prediction is written down so the run can falsify it, not confirm it.
The standing direction holds: no conclusion from fewer than five clean
trials per cell.

## The refinement (the operator, 2026-10-30) — structure must *promote* the walk

> The only way we can prove that the structure will be the model that's
> more superior with a bare environment to a structured environment is if
> we play on that inclination to explore outside — and the structure that
> the lesser model sits in encourages and promotes that exploration
> outside.

This sharpens the mechanism from *structure substitutes for exploration*
to *structure that wins is structure that induces it*. A rulebook contract
(the polar seed's `AGENTS.md` as committed: the code is the authority,
compute with a script, validate, commit) constrains; its one exploratory
push is "walk every thing that rests on the changed clause." If the
refinement is right, a seed variant whose contract says *before you
compute, read what links here and what it rests on* should lift the small
model's changed-set figures **and** its read set together — and a variant
that constrains harder without pushing the walk should not.

Two things followed the same day, so the refinement is testable rather
than a reading:

1. **The read set is now a recorded surface.** The runner captures every
   tool call (`stream-json`) and records per session what the agent read
   in the workspace, what it read in the framework view, and any path it
   reached outside both. "Haiku under structure explores like opus bare"
   becomes a comparison of two numbers per cell.
2. **The seed's contract is a declared moving surface** of the
   `eval-cycle` definition, and cycle 3 of the polar programme moves it
   alone: same model, same fixture, same framework — only how hard the
   contract pushes the walk.

The prediction this adds to the list above: on the framework arm, the
small model's **changed-set pass rate tracks its read set** across
contract variants; on the bare arm the flagship's read set is large
without being asked. Where the read set rises and the figures do not, the
walk is not the mechanism and this insight falls.
