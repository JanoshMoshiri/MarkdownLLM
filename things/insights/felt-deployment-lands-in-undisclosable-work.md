# The Framework's Felt Evidence Lands in Undisclosable Work

## The Insight

"Deploy when felt" is the framework's discipline for when a primitive graduates
from *specified* to *load-bearing*: you don't deploy a mechanism on schedule, you
deploy it when a real situation demands it. But the operator's felt-trigger fires
overwhelmingly inside **confidential professional work** — domains built in the
course of employment at a law firm, owned by the firm as client IP. That work is
genuine framework deployment, and it **cannot enter the public repo**.

Two consequences follow, and the second is the durable one:

1. **The public repo systematically undercounts deployment.** Items the public
   tracking shows as "deferred" or "not yet done" are frequently *done* — exercised
   on real private domains — just invisibly. The `workflow-run` primitive's first
   live use ("a real domain, private IP, not in the public corpus") was recorded as
   a one-off; it is the **rule, not the exception**. Change-reconciliation on a
   twisted domain, the workflow primitives, the felt parts of the manifesto's claims
   — these are being lived, off-camera.

2. **The public repo cannot be the evidence base for felt-trigger items.** This is
   *why* the evals are synthetic (`sleeping-bag-fac`, `vat-quarter-basic`): a
   disclosable proxy was built precisely because the real domains are confidential.
   The synthetic eval is the public stand-in for evidence that exists but can't be
   shown ([[structure-decides-figures-scale-decides-convention]]).

The **cold-start human eval** is the canonical case. It has happened — informally,
with the operator's brother — but a clean, sourced, *disclosable* writeup is not
producible right now and may never be. Tracking it as "the undone centrepiece" was
a category error: it conflated *not done* with *done but undisclosable*.

## Why It Matters

Left uncorrected, the framework's own tracking nags indefinitely about resolved
work and mis-reads the framework's maturity from the public surface alone. A reader
(or a future session) sees a pile of "deferred / felt-when-felt" threads and
concludes the framework is unproven, when in fact its strongest real-world use is
simply behind a confidentiality wall.

## How To Apply

- When a thread is in the **felt-trigger / private-deployment** category, mark it
  *exercised privately; public artifact unavailable* and stop re-surfacing it as
  open work. It is closed for tracking purposes even though no public commit shows it.
- Reserve **open-thread** status for things genuinely not done, *or* for the
  **disclosable-proxy** work that legitimately can be public — synthetic evals,
  sanitised case studies, `limitations.md`. Those are the real public backlog; the
  private deployments are not.
- Do not read the public repo's silence as the framework's immaturity. The honest
  public claim is "proven on confidential domains; demonstrated publicly via
  synthetic fixtures," not "awaiting first real use."
- This generalises the standing call (2026-06-17) to *not record pre-floor adoption
  anecdotes*: undisclosable evidence is kept out of the corpus deliberately, not
  banked as unverifiable testimony — but it is still acknowledged as real.
