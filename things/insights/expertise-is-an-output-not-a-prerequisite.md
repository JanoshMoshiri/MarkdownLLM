# Expertise Is an Output of the System, Not a Prerequisite of the Operator

## The Insight

Read what the floor actually demands of its human: quarantine says no output
rests on an external thing *until a human confirms*; the flip names a person
in `verified_by`. Nowhere — not in provenance, not in the kernel, not in any
spec — does the framework ask for a credential. It asks for **authority**: a
human with the standing to say "this is true in my domain." For a regulated
quality system that standing happens to require a profession. For a person's
own spending, calendar, or household, the standing is automatic and absolute
— no professional on earth knows which transaction was a gift and which was a
mistake. The everyday operator is not a degraded case of the expert operator;
they are the *purest* case of what the substrate actually requires.

## The Inverted Arrow

Classic expert systems encoded an expert's knowledge so non-experts could
consult it: expertise in, answers out. This substrate runs the arrow
backwards. The operator brings authority and intent; the substrate brings
discipline; and **expertise accumulates in the domain as a product of use** —
insights with dispositions, decisions with rationale, the graph of what was
learned and why it still holds. The expert-accumulator role does not require
an expert operator; it *manufactures* domain expertise from operation. A
personal domain, after enough use, knows things about its subject that no
professional knows and no generic tool could — it becomes the only expert on
its subject in existence.

## The Naming Arc, Resolved

The framework's own description drifted: *definition-driven domain system* →
*expert system* → the word "expert" removed again. That wasn't indecision —
"expert" smuggles in "professional," which misstates the requirement
(authority) and misplaces the expertise (it lives in the domain, not the
operator). Definition-driven was right all along; *expert* names an emergent
property of the domain. Where the seed expertise comes from differs by case —
a professional seeds a regulated domain from their training; a personal
domain grows its expertise from nothing but use — and the machinery never
asks which case it is in.
