# Mesh Safety Is the Floor, Not the Topology

## The Tension

The operator's held view, stated 2026-09-22: the risk in self-modifying
agents traces to *"the one-track mind of the orchestrator driving the
sub-agents"* — a tree with a single point of authority — and the answer is
the organism: autonomous peer domains, each with its membrane, memory and
identity, coordinating through committed state rather than command.

The agent's counter-position, offered and not ruled on: **topology is not
the load-bearing safety variable.** A self-modifying agent is dangerous in a
mesh too. What the framework actually does about risk is different from
"peers instead of hierarchy" and better than it:

- the beings do not modify *themselves* — they modify their **record**;
- every modification is committed and floor-validated;
- inflections are reconciled against dependents (cues);
- the irreversible act goes to a human seat, by construction.

That is self-modification under a constitution the being does not own. The
mesh is what the constitution *enables*. The safety is the floor and the
seat.

## Why It Matters

Two consequences follow if the counter-position is right, and both bear on
the next arc — an estate of six to eight domains passing work between them:

1. **A peer mesh is exactly as safe as its mechanical floor and not one inch
   safer.** The framework's own evidence — five sessions across three
   harnesses skipping an interpretation-anchored Tier-0 rule — says a rule
   agents are *asked* to honour is skippable. Distributing authority to
   human seats is a real property of the shape, but it holds only where the
   seat is reached by mechanism, not by prose.
2. **Topology arguments will recur, and they will feel like safety
   arguments.** "The mesh has no single point of failure" is true and is
   about availability. "The mesh has no single point of authority" is true
   and is about *where* authority sits, not whether it is honoured. Neither
   is a claim about what an agent can do to state it can reach.

There is one thing the topology genuinely buys, and it should be named so
the insight is not read as dismissing the vision: in a tree, one root agent
holds authority over every child; in a mesh with a human at each
irreversible, no agent holds authority over another. That is a distribution
of *human* authority, and it is only as good as the floor that routes the
irreversible to the seat.

## Status

A position, argued once, not ruled. Medium confidence. Kept because the
operator is about to build the body this is about, and a fresh agent should
know the record holds both readings and which one the floor's evidence
supports. **Dismissal condition:** the operator rules that topology is the
safety variable and says why the anchor evidence does not apply.
**Promotion:** into the manifesto's principle 7 (inspectable, not autonomous)
or `operating-model.md`, once a second domain body is composed and the seats
are audited against it.
