# The Rough, True Account Is Generative Infrastructure, Not a Confession

## The Insight

A record of how work actually went — including the dead ends, the reversals, and
the overreaches — is not a confession to be minimised. It is **infrastructure**:
it is what the next builder stands on. A polished narrative that shows only the
finished surface deletes exactly the information someone needs to extend the work,
because the most load-bearing knowledge is usually *negative* — what was tried and
abandoned, and **why**.

Transparency, in other words, is **forward-looking**. The instinct to smooth a
record reads it as backward-looking — *how do I look, having finished?* But its
real audience is ahead of it: the person, or the next agent, who picks the work up
and tries to push it further. To them, "we built X and then killed it because Y"
is worth more than X ever pretending it was never built.

## Why It Matters

Three concrete ways a true account out-builds a polished one:

- **Reversals are the highest-value records.** Knowing an approach was tried and
  *why it was rejected* saves the next person from re-deriving the dead end.
  (Worked case, this session: the retired-vocabulary register was built, then
  reverted because a check needing a hand-maintained suppression list isn't
  floor-shaped — `judgement-checks-need-a-suppression-list-which-is-itself-drift`.
  A polished history would show "considered, decided against" or nothing; the true
  record hands over the whole reasoning, so the next builder doesn't rebuild it.)
- **Honest gaps mark where the risk lives.** Recording that v3.17.0 was published
  before the reconciliation Walk reached the insight corpus does not just admit a
  miss — it tells future work *where drift hides after an inflection*. The mistake,
  recorded, becomes a map.
- **It compounds across people and agents.** This is the multiplier: a community,
  or a fleet of agents, can only build on what they can *see*. They inherit the
  reasoning, not just the artefact — so the boundary they start from is the true
  one, seams and all, not a flattering reconstruction they have to reverse-engineer
  before they can push past it.

This is the *why* under mechanisms the framework already has — provenance chains,
[belief-revision](../../belief-revision.md) recording superseded beliefs, the
CHANGELOG recording reversals and not just releases, insights that capture dead
ends. None of them are confessional bookkeeping; they are the framework choosing a
**true** record over a clean-looking one, because the true one is the one you can
build on. The worth of the corpus is not that it is polished — it is that it is
honest.
