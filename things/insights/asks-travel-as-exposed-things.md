# Asks Travel as Exposed Things

## The Insight

A domain that needs something *from* another domain does not message it,
write into it, or register itself anywhere — it **writes the ask as a thing,
exposes it on its own porch, and lets the addressee pull it.** The membrane's
one-way discipline survives intact: the asker publishes to its own face
(which it may always do); the addressee reads a face (which it may always
do). A request is just content whose subject is the reader.

Validated end-to-end 2026-08-05: a consumer estate's vantage domain exposed
a four-ask build brief with evidence, re-verification dates, and its own
chase triggers; the framework pulled it and shipped all four asks as
v3.27.0 within the day. The membrane carried its own bug reports upstream —
including one (the all-digit pin coercion) that the framework's own CI
independently confirmed within hours, two domains having previously patched
it locally with no shared knowledge of each other.

## The Caveat That Makes It Honest

An exposed ask is a letter in a mailbox the addressee must actually watch.
The framework had no `.mcp.json` route to any consumer porch — the pointer
arrived by operator voice ("go and check the porch manually"). The pattern's
integrity therefore rests on one of: an addressee-side route plus a standing
check, or the asker's **dated chase trigger** (the brief carried one:
hand-off chase at +14 days — "a brief nobody pulled is indistinguishable
from one nobody needed"). The chase trigger is not decoration; it is the
half of the mechanism that survives an unwatched mailbox.

## When To Reach For It

Any upstream want: a bug report to the framework, a schema request to a
source domain, a review ask to an oversight domain. The alternative —
telling the addressee directly — works exactly once and leaves no record,
no pin, no chase, and no quarantine discipline on what was claimed.
