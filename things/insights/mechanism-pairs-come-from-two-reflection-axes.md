# The Floor's Mechanism Pairs Are Generated By Two Reflection Axes

## The Insight

The recurring experience of building a mechanism and then discovering its
"other side of the coin" is not a series of coincidences — it is the surface of
two orthogonal **reflection symmetries** the framework is built on, plus a third
that runs through both as a division of labour. Name them and the pairs stop
being surprises and become a checklist:

- **Spatial axis — inbound ↔ outbound** (direction *through the relationship
  graph*, at a change boundary). `touchpoints` (what points at me / what did I
  risk) ↔ `cascade` (what I unblock / what I completed forward); the
  `relationships` + reverse-`provenance` indexes (inbound cache) ↔ reading a
  thing's own frontmatter (outbound). This axis is exactly
  `directional-graph-reads-come-in-inbound-outbound-pairs`.
- **Temporal axis — forward ↔ backward** (direction *through time*).
  Change-reconciliation at-change (forward, delta-scoped, human-cued) ↔
  retrospective reconciliation (backward, full-corpus, reconstructed from git).
  Continuity brief (prospective) ↔ WORKLOG (retrospective) — *both retired at
  v3.17; the pair was real, and its roles now live in session-start orientation
  (forward) ↔ the `mdllm worklog` view (backward), which is the symmetry
  surviving its instances.* Consistency *prevented* at write-time ↔ contradiction
  *created* at change-time.
- **Labour axis — mechanical ↔ semantic** (who does it), running through every
  quadrant: floor validation ↔ the Walk; "never re-perform a mechanical check by
  reasoning."

Almost every framework mechanism is one quadrant of (inbound/outbound) ×
(forward/backward), with the labour axis deciding floor-vs-agent within the cell.
`composition-is-the-inverse-of-decomposition` is the same shape on the cohesion
operation; `directional-graph-reads` is the same shape on the graph read. They
are not separate discoveries — they are this principle seen from two angles.

The durable rule: **when you build a mechanism, locate its quadrant and check the
mirror across each axis before assuming the job is done.** A mechanism that
exists in only one cell of a pair is a half-built feature, not a finished one —
and the empty cell is usually equally useful (the under-building error from
`directional-graph-reads`).

## Why It Matters

It converts a felt pattern into a *generator*. Instead of stumbling onto
counterparts one at a time, the two axes let you enumerate them: for any new
floor affordance, ask "what is its inbound/outbound mirror? its forward/backward
mirror? its mechanical/semantic split?" — and the gaps fall out by construction
rather than by accident months later. It is the meta-razor behind the specific
ones: `directional-graph-reads` (spatial) and the prevented/created framing of
`change-reconciliation` (temporal) are both corollaries of it.

It is also the natural spine for a *symmetry/coverage view* of the framework map:
the three existing views are topological (elevation, spec edges, subcommand→spec);
this principle is the operational view — every mechanism placed in its quadrant,
and any empty cell drawn as a visible hole. The first hole it exposes is captured
separately as [[cross-domain-handoff-is-built-inbound-only]].

## Context

Surfaced 2026-06-24 during an operator-requested floor review "from the
two-sides-of-the-coin perspective." The review found the operator was tracking
*two* symmetries as if they were one; separating the spatial and temporal axes
(with labour as the third) explained why the counterpart moments kept recurring
and turned the observation into a forward-looking design check. Cool because it
is reflexive: the framework's own mechanisms obey a small symmetry group, and
naming the group predicts where the next pair — or the next gap — will be.
