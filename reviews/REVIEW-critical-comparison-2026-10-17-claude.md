# MarkdownLLM Recombines Old Parts, Overclaims Coherence

*Provenance: commissioned by the framework author on 2026-10-17 and written by a model — a Claude Fable 5.1 coordinator session dispatched six research sessions (agent-memory libraries; harness- and product-native memory; file-based markdown/git patterns; the formal and academic lineage; an internal audit of this repository at HEAD `2c95b3f`; an adversarial prior-art prosecution) and one writer. Web sources were read on 2026-10-17. No human reviewer has verified it; it is recorded as model-authored, which is what its own last recommendation asks of every file in this folder.*

MarkdownLLM is not reaching too far in what it has built; it is reaching too far in how it describes what it has built. Judged against the dedicated memory libraries (Mem0, Zep/Graphiti, Letta), the harness-native layers (Claude Code, Copilot Memory, Codex), the file-based patterns (Karpathy's LLM wiki, Cline's Memory Bank, Basic Memory, spec-kit/Kiro, Backstage, org-mode) and the formal lineage (Doyle's truth maintenance, AGM belief revision, W3C PROV, OpenGitOps, event sourcing, Wikidata constraints), **five of its seven "Key Innovations" are repackaged prior art, two are recombinations, and none of its eight load-bearing mechanisms is novel in isolation** — five are repackaged, three are recombinations. What survives is narrow and real: a pre-commit gate that refuses a definition-surface commit until a dependant walk is recorded, a reconciliation question that persists as a file until a human rules, decision records pinned to commit SHAs with a reverse-provenance index, an explicitly stated floor/agent/human allocation, and a framework that validates its own specifications with the floor it imposes on domains. None of these is what the README leads with, and none has been exercised by anyone outside the author's estate. The README's lead claim — that other tools "store and retrieve" while MarkdownLLM alone asks "is any of it still true" — is overstated: Zep's bi-temporal invalidation, Copilot Memory's read-time re-verification against the branch, Karpathy's lint, Letta's consolidation and a 2026 governance literature all address that question; MarkdownLLM's actual distinction is that it refuses to let an agent settle the question silently. The evidence base is thin on every axis the field uses: **one author wrote 98.7% of 1,017 commits**, all 17 "independent" reviews were LLM sessions the author commissioned, the one eval saturated and was contaminated, and the corpus's own October retrospective records **173 answered reconciliation cues in five weeks**, **thirteen stalled high-priority plans**, and a **90-minute onboarding wall** for the only non-author operator. The 2025–2026 convergence of Letta, Anthropic, GitHub and EverMind on files, git and verification validates the direction MarkdownLLM chose; it does not validate MarkdownLLM. Its lead on three narrow mechanisms is measured in months, its vocabulary hides how much it borrowed, and the Claude Code channel it chose to deliver its "emitted floor" cuts that floor at 10,000 characters.

*Scope and sourcing: repository facts are read at HEAD `2c95b3f` (framework 3.48.0, 2026-10-17) and cited by root-relative path; external facts are cited to the page read on 2026-10-17. Private workspaces and non-author people are described generically throughout.*

## Three verdicts, before the evidence

**Am I reaching too far? Yes, in three specific places, and no everywhere else.** The three overreaches are textual, not architectural. First, the scale claim: `AGENTS.md` says the three-layer pattern "works from 10 things to 10,000" while the framework's own `scalability-guide.md` says contextual loading "Works up to 200-300 things in a single domain" and that beyond "1,000 active things" the operator should "consider whether this framework's philosophy — full LLM reasoning over unindexed data — is the right fit" ([AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md); [scalability-guide.md](../scalability-guide.md)). Both cannot be true; the guide is the measured document. Second, the README's lead positioning (judged in its own section below). Third, the label "Key Innovations" on a list that is, item by item, the AGENTS.md convention, de Bono's hats, progressive disclosure, few-shot examples, a gitignored inner repo, a scale claim the guide contradicts, and a GitOps reconciliation loop. The architecture itself does not overreach: it declines to automate semantic consistency (which Eiter and Gottlob proved intractable beyond NP for propositional bases), it bounds the dependant walk (as Forbus had to bound the ATMS), and it routes irreversible judgement to a human — the position the 2026 governance literature is recommending rather than the one it is testing ([Eiter & Gottlob 1992](https://www.cs.ox.ac.uk/publications/publication286-abstract.html); [Forbus 1988](https://qrg.northwestern.edu/papers/Files/QRG_Dist_Files/QRG_1988/Forbus_1988_Focusing_the_ATMS.pdf); [Always-On Agents survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30306)).

**Does this have a special quality? Yes, and it is narrower than the README says and better documented than any competitor's.** The special quality is not coherence, not markdown, not git, and not the 42-subcommand CLI. It is that no surveyed system lets a deterministic check refuse a commit because a definition changed and nobody has yet recorded what depends on it, and no surveyed system keeps the resulting question as a first-class record until a human writes a verdict ([Böckeler on Kiro/spec-kit/Tessl](https://www.martinfowler.com/articles/exploring-gen-ai/sdd-3-tools.html); [gbrain frontmatter-guard](https://skills.sh/garrytan/gbrain/frontmatter-guard); [Mem0 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.19413)). A second, less flattering special quality is candour: the repository's own retrospective, insight files and commissioned reviews document its defects more precisely than this report could from outside — "in-progress has become the status of record for work nobody is doing" is the corpus's sentence, not a reviewer's ([things/retrospectives/framework-retrospective-2026-10.md](../things/retrospectives/framework-retrospective-2026-10.md)). That candour is unusual in a field where a vendor retracted a "100% on LoCoMo" claim within days of publishing it ([Bloo-mind](https://essays.bloo-mind.ai/posts/2026-05-20-mem-eval/)). But candour recorded in files that nobody outside the estate reads is not evidence that the mechanisms work for anyone else.

**Is it repeating something already done? Mostly, yes — as recombination, not copy.** Every element has a named predecessor with ten to forty-five years of literature: typed frontmatter validated at a boundary (Astro content collections; MADR), tool-evaluated deadlines and dependency blocking (org-mode), LLM-maintained wiki with lint (Karpathy, April 2026), decision records (ADRs), provenance pins (PROV, DVC, Nix), suspect links on dependants (IBM DOORS), truth maintenance (Doyle 1979), git as event stream (Fowler 2005), continuous reconciliation from a versioned source (OpenGitOps), bot-generated constraint-violation reports (Wikidata) ([file-based patterns synthesis](https://docs.astro.build/en/guides/content-collections/); [Org manual](https://orgmode.org/manual/TODO-dependencies.html); [Karpathy gist](https://gist.github.com/karpathy/442a6bf555914893e9891c11519de94f); [W3C PROV-DM](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-dm/); [DOORS suspect links](https://jazz.net/library/article/88104); [Doyle TMS](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/6287); [Fowler](https://martinfowler.com/eaaDev/EventSourcing.html); [OpenGitOps](https://opengitops.dev/); [Wikidata constraints](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Help:Property_constraints_portal)). The assembly of all of them under one validator, with an LLM as the primary writer and a human as the residual judge, was not found anywhere. The manifesto already says this — "The framework's contribution is the pattern of how these pieces compose — not the pieces themselves" — and the README should say it too ([llm-driven-systems.manifesto.md](../llm-driven-systems.manifesto.md)).

Three things stand out in the matrix. Every system except MarkdownLLM, Backstage and org-mode leaves the write path to an LLM with no boundary check at all, and the two 2026 field studies that looked at the write path found it is where systems break: weaker backbones produced "Silent Failure" of memory writes with format errors rising from 17.9% to 30.4%, and agents handed a consolidated-but-superseded constraint re-verified it in about one episode in five and acted on the stale version in 74.7–77.3% of episodes ([Anatomy of Agentic Memory](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.19320v2); [arXiv 2608.25553](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25553)). Second, the benchmark column is unusable for comparison: Penfield's audit found **6.4% of LoCoMo's answer key wrong** (a perfect system scores ~93.6%) and the standard judge accepting **62.81% of deliberately wrong answers**, Bench'd's methodology-constant harness put most memory layers at or below a **57.6% no-memory baseline**, and Mem0's own 2025 table had full-context (72.90) beating every memory system ([Penfield](https://dev.to/penfieldlabs/we-audited-locomo-64-of-the-answer-key-is-wrong-and-the-judge-accepts-up-to-63-of-intentionally-33lg); [Bench'd](https://benchd.ai/benchmarks); [Mem0 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.19413)). MarkdownLLM's refusal to publish a conclusion from a saturated fixture is more honest than the field norm — and leaves it with no number at all. Third, the adoption column is where the asymmetry is brutal: the systems with the weakest governance have the users, and the one with the strongest governance has one.

The pattern across the table is that vocabulary does the work the architecture does not. The eight coined terms appear roughly 2,000 times across the root specs and docs — **thing 995, floor 363, estate 244, walk 168, cue 152, seat 46, porch 34, reckoning 28** — and no glossary file exists in the repository ([README.md](../README.md); [docs/](../docs/)). Every competitor names the same ideas in industry-standard words: drift / sync / reconcile (Argo), suspect links / impact analysis (DOORS), ADR, memory blocks (Letta), lint / ingest (Karpathy), steering / constitution (Kiro, spec-kit), `valid_at` / `invalid_at` (Zep). The cost is threefold: onboarding time (the 90-minute wall below), zero search discoverability for anyone comparing against Backstage or Argo, and — the cost that matters to this review — a private register that hides how much is prior art and inflates apparent novelty. A reader who meets "the floor refuses the commit until the walk is on record" reasonably assumes something new; a reader who meets "a pre-commit validator refuses the commit until the suspect-link review is recorded" correctly recognises DOORS transplanted to git, and can then appreciate the transplant on its merits.

Where the prosecution fails, it fails in three places and should say so. First, three mechanisms do combine known parts in a way nobody else has shipped — the cue gate, the commit-pinned decision records with a reverse index, and the reckoning's forced disposition — and a fair external reviewer would credit them as a small, real contribution to agent-memory engineering. Second, the field's own tools vindicate the floor's existence: Claude Code's documentation now says "Unlike CLAUDE.md instructions which are advisory, hooks are deterministic", Anthropic's long-running-agent harness chose a JSON feature list over Markdown because "the model is less likely to inappropriately change it", and GitHub's engineers list "outdated, incorrect, or even maliciously injected memories" as their main worry ([best practices](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices); [Anthropic harnesses](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-harnesses-for-long-running-agents); [GitHub blog](https://github.blog/2026-01-09-building-an-agentic-memory-system-for-github-copilot)). The vendors' modal remedy for "ignored instruction" is to move it out of prose into enforcement — which is the floor's thesis. Third, the formal lineage says the choice *not* to automate semantic reconciliation is the correct engineering response, not a gap: belief-base revision is beyond NP in general, iterated revision is unsettled forty years after AGM, and the ATMS had to be "focused" to survive its own closure computation ([Eiter & Gottlob](https://www.cs.ox.ac.uk/publications/publication286-abstract.html); [SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/logic-belief-revision/); [Forbus](https://qrg.northwestern.edu/papers/Files/QRG_Dist_Files/QRG_1988/Forbus_1988_Focusing_the_ATMS.pdf)).

## The README's lead claim describes what competitors already do

The README's lead reads: "Plenty of tools already do the storing and the retrieving: Obsidian vaults, LLM wiki, file lookups. What they leave open is what happens after hundreds of sessions and a mountain of saved memory — when the question stops being *how do I retrieve this* and becomes *is any of it still true*. That's what MarkdownLLM is about: not storing more, but keeping what's stored coherent" ([README.md](../README.md)). Judged against the field, this is overstated in two ways: by the comparison set it names, and by the verb "leave open".

The comparison set is the overstatement by omission. Obsidian vaults and file lookups are strawmen; the systems that actually compete on the "is it still true" question are not mentioned anywhere in the repository — a grep for Mem0, Zep, Letta, MemGPT, Karpathy, Memory Bank, Cline, org-mode, LangChain, Graphiti and Cognee across every `.md` outside private workspaces returns **zero hits** ([internal audit, Q5](../README.md)). The one near-contemporary the README does gesture at, "LLM wiki", is unattributed. The README's own commissioned reviews asked for the comparison four times from 2026-06-11 — "the obvious question from any newcomer is 'why not just CLAUDE.md + a notes folder?' and the corpus never answers it directly" — and the eval backlog still lists "the 'why not CLAUDE.md + a notes folder' answer the corpus could win and never makes" ([reviews/REVIEW-independent-2026-06-11.md](../reviews/REVIEW-independent-2026-06-11.md); [things/plans/evidence-and-eval-backlog.md](../things/plans/evidence-and-eval-backlog.md)).

The verb "leave open" is the overstatement by assertion. Zep's Graphiti is bi-temporal — every fact edge carries `valid_at`/`invalid_at` for when it was true in the world and `created_at`/`expired_at` for when the system knew it; "When information changes, old facts are invalidated — not deleted" ([Zep paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.13956); [Graphiti README](https://github.com/getzep/graphiti)). For atomic facts this is a more rigorous answer to "is it still true" than MarkdownLLM's `status` / `stale` / `supersedes` vocabulary, which does not distinguish real-world validity from system knowledge. Mem0's 2025 paper mechanised the question as an LLM choosing ADD / UPDATE / DELETE / NOOP against the top similar memories — and then, in April 2026, abandoned it for "Single-pass ADD-only extraction… Memories accumulate; nothing is overwritten", listing "memory staleness" among the problems "left to application-layer decisions" ([Mem0 paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.19413); [Mem0 README](https://github.com/mem0ai/mem0); [Mem0 blog](https://mem0.ai/blog/state-of-ai-agent-memory-2026)). That retreat is the strongest evidence in this review that the problem the README names is real and that an LLM-only resolver does not survive scale — it supports MarkdownLLM's thesis while contradicting the README's claim that nobody addressed it. Letta's MemGPT made self-editing memory the whole point in 2023 and in 2026 rebuilt it as git-backed markdown with "dreaming" subagents that "review recent conversations, consolidate useful lessons, and update memory" — without asking approval ([MemGPT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.08560v1); [Letta memory docs](https://docs.letta.com/letta-code/memory)). Karpathy's lint names the question almost verbatim: "contradictions between pages, stale claims that newer sources have superseded, orphan pages with no inbound links" ([gist](https://gist.github.com/karpathy/442a6bf555914893e9891c11519de94f)). Copilot Memory answers it at read time: each repository fact is stored "with citations pointing to the code that supports them", and before use the agent "checks those citations against the current branch to confirm the information is still accurate. Only validated facts are used"; GitHub explicitly rejected an offline curation service as "significant engineering complexity and LLM costs" ([Copilot Memory docs](https://docs.github.com/en/enterprise-cloud@latest/copilot/concepts/agents/copilot-memory); [GitHub blog](https://github.blog/2026-01-09-building-an-agentic-memory-system-for-github-copilot)). And the 2026 governance literature is about nothing else: STALE benchmarks "Implicit Conflict" and finds the best model at **55.2%**; the stale-constraints paper shows agents acting on superseded constraints in roughly three episodes of four; the memory-security survey proposes W3C PROV as the store-stage serialisation plus "Write Authorization" with "human or policy arbitration"; the schema-grounded-memory paper argues memory should be "a system of record" with "values that must never be inferred" ([STALE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06527); [arXiv 2608.25553](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25553); [security survey](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.16548); [arXiv 2604.27906](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.27906)).

What MarkdownLLM actually does differently is narrower and more interesting than "keeps what's stored coherent". Every system above resolves the question by LLM judgement at write time (Zep, Mem0-2025, Letta, Karpathy) or by mechanical re-verification at read time (Copilot). MarkdownLLM is the only one surveyed in which the resolution of a contradiction or a definition change is routed to a human verdict, recorded as a file, and refused passage at the commit boundary until that record exists. It is also the only one whose history is the full git log rather than a 28-day window (Copilot), 30 days of versions (Anthropic's memory stores), or an auto-generated commit message (Letta). The narrower claim that would be true: *"Other systems let a model decide what is still true, or check it when it is next read. MarkdownLLM refuses to let a model settle that question silently: a change to a definition raises a recorded question over everything that depends on it, the agent walks it, a human rules on what the agent may not settle, and the ruling is committed. The history is git, not a retention window."* That sentence is defensible from the record. The current lead sentence is not, and it is also the sentence the framework has never measured — the manifesto concedes the longitudinal half "is designed but not yet measured by a multi-session eval" ([llm-driven-systems.manifesto.md](../llm-driven-systems.manifesto.md)). The repository's `AGENTS.md` principles and manifesto hedges already narrowed every strong claim after the August 2026 substrate review ("It overreaches when it converts that discipline into empirical or universal guarantees"); the README lead is the one surface that did not catch up ([reviews/REVIEW-independent-substrate-2026-08-20-codex.md](../reviews/REVIEW-independent-substrate-2026-08-20-codex.md)).

### The delivery layer is cut on exactly the channel meant to carry it

The fresh evidence deserves its own paragraph because it is the sharpest weakness and the framework predicted it. Version 3.33 made the kernel "Delivered by emission, not instruction" on the grounds — recorded in an insight — that "Content *emitted* into the transcript is consumed; content the entry file *instructs the agent to load* is economised — skipped until something makes it the pulled thing" ([AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md); [emitted-content-is-read-instructed-content-is-economised.md](../things/insights/emitted-content-is-read-instructed-content-is-economised.md)). The design acknowledged that the Claude Code hook channel is budgeted: there the kernel is "loudly deferred with the same integrity facts, and the read it names is owed before acting on domain state". On 2026-10-17 the author observed in a live session that even the digest — not the kernel, the digest — arrived with repeated `[truncated]` markers and that the agent is never visibly encouraged to read the rest. The mechanics are documented by the vendor: a hook's `additionalContext` is capped at 10,000 characters, overflow is saved to a file and replaced by a path plus a 2,000-character preview, and "Claude Code doesn't ask Claude to read the file" ([hooks reference](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/hooks)). By the tool's own chars/3.8 heuristic the kernel alone is roughly 22,000 characters — more than twice the cap — and `AGENTS.md` roughly 36,000. A third-party proxy capture additionally reports that Claude Code injects "You should not respond to this context unless it is highly relevant to your task" alongside injected context, which, if accurate, actively discounts whatever does arrive ([HumanLayer](https://www.humanlayer.dev/blog/writing-a-good-claude-md); third-party, not vendor-confirmed).

The bind is exact. The channel that reliably delivers (emission) is capped below the floor's size; the remainder must be instructed; the framework's own record says instructed content is economised. So on the harness the framework runs on most, "Tier 0 is emitted or owed, never economised" is true only of the first 10,000 characters, and the owed read falls to a reader the corpus has watched skip it on "the strongest available tier". This is not the framework's design fault — the cap is Claude Code's, and the vendor's identical issue has been auto-closed — but it is the framework's choice of channel, and `session-start-hardening` Phase 5, the live re-test on two harnesses that would have caught it, has sat at "0 of 9 boxes after 48 days" ([things/plans/session-start-hardening.md](../things/plans/session-start-hardening.md); [framework-retrospective-2026-10.md](../things/retrospectives/framework-retrospective-2026-10.md)). The practical consequence is that the one surface the framework claims is "exempt from the economy rule" is, on this channel, the most economised surface it has.

### The lineage predicted every one of these

None of the weaknesses above is a surprise to the fields MarkdownLLM borrowed from; each is the standard failure of its ancestor. Cyc took "a person-century of effort" for ~10^5 concepts, changed its representation language mid-project, and had to decontextualise every assertion and then re-tag each with its contexts — the large-scale form of the schema prune and the relation drift `AGENTS.md` records ([Lenat 1995](https://papers.baulab.info/papers/knowledge/Lenat-1995.pdf)). Gill's study of the first commercial expert systems found most fell into disuse not through technical failure but "problems in transitioning from development to maintenance" and lack of user acceptance — the second-seat and partner-onboarding problem ([Gill 1995](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/410061)). Halfaker and colleagues tied English Wikipedia's decline to rising newcomer rejection and "the growth of bots and algorithmic quality control tools" — a pre-commit gate that refuses commits protects quality and repels the newcomer, which is what the 90 minutes measured ([Halfaker et al. 2013](https://stuartgeiger.com/papers/abs-rise-and-decline-wikipedia.pdf)). Wikidata found that "a small portion of the constraints yields a large portion of violations" and that modifying constraint definitions, not data, reduces recurring violations — the rules themselves need repair, which is what 135 not-inflection cues out of 173 are telling the framework about the cue carrier's trigger ([Shenoy et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.00156); [Ferranti et al. ISWC 2025](https://aic.ai.wu.ac.at/~polleres/publications/ferr-etal-2025ISWC.pdf)). FlaggedRevs' human review queues reached a month and a half, averaging 15 days — the 39-day unverified review ([Meta FlaggedRevs](https://meta.wikimedia.org/wiki/FlaggedRevs)). And Young's warning that whole-system event sourcing is "a really big failure" is the warning against making every interaction a thing — which 173 answered cue files are ([InfoQ 2016](https://www.infoq.com/news/2016/04/event-sourcing-anti-pattern)). The one lesson the lineage offers in the framework's favour is also the strongest: the 2026 stale-constraints result that agents will not spontaneously re-verify a settled-looking definition is the empirical case for mechanically raised reconciliation cues rather than relying on the agent to notice ([arXiv 2608.25553](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25553)).

## What genuinely has no found precedent is narrow and unvalidated

Five items survive the prosecution. Each is bounded by public sources — closed products such as Letta Cloud and Zep Cloud could not be inspected — and each is stated as "not found in surveyed prior art", not "does not exist".

The first is the pre-commit gate that refuses a definition-surface commit until a dependant walk is recorded. DOORS suspect links are the idea — a persistent flag on linked artefacts "that need re-evaluation and possible modification because a linked artifact changed", cleared by human review, feeding impact analysis — but they live inside a requirements tool, not at a version-control commit boundary, and nothing in the surveyed spec-driven tools (Kiro, spec-kit, Tessl) performs commit-time validation or drift detection at all ([Jazz Library](https://jazz.net/library/article/88104); [Böckeler](https://www.martinfowler.com/articles/exploring-gen-ai/sdd-3-tools.html)). gbrain's hook validates frontmatter mechanics, not dependants ([gbrain](https://skills.sh/garrytan/gbrain/frontmatter-guard)). The second is `type: cue` as a first-class record that the session-start digest re-lists until a human verdict is written. Karpathy's lint reports contradictions and forgets them; Mem0 has no audit trail and no human step; Zep retains invalidated edges but has no human step; Claude Code's `/doctor prompt-audit` proposes and "changes nothing until asked" but keeps no record of the question ([gist](https://gist.github.com/karpathy/442a6bf555914893e9891c11519de94f); [Mem0 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.19413); [Zep paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.13956v1); [Claude Code memory](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/memory)). The third is decision records whose `informed_by` inputs are pinned to full commit SHAs, with a reverse-provenance index enabling diff-driven regeneration of outputs. ADRs supply the shape, PROV the relations, DVC and Nix the hash pins; an independent September 2026 post argues the same shape for Obsidian plus Claude Code but as a workflow, not a mechanism; no single tool was found doing all four for an LLM agent's outputs ([PROV-DM](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-dm/); [EvoMap](https://evomap.ai/blog/obsidian-claude-code-vault-to-code-workflow)). The fourth is the allocation principle stated as architecture: the floor enforces only enumerated structural checks, the agent reasons over meaning, and every irreversible or unsettleable ruling is routed to a human "residue" — "Never re-perform mechanical checks by reasoning" and "when a move's consequence could not be recovered after the fact… that judgement belongs to the human and to the structure, not to a prediction of yours" ([kernel.md](../kernel.md); [AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md)). The 2026 surveys call for exactly this allocation — "human or policy arbitration", a floor between "what must be remembered" and "which values must never be inferred" — but as recommendation, not as a shipped division of labour ([security survey](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.16548); [arXiv 2604.27906](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.27906)). The fifth is self-description: the framework's specifications are things validated by the same floor that validates domains, and `mdllm coherence` fails the commit when a generated section drifts from its source — Knuth's tangle applied to the spec corpus ([Knuth WEB](https://www.cs.tufts.edu/~nr/cs257/archive/don-knuth/web.pdf)).

Three qualifications apply to all five. They are narrow: items one to three are mechanisms a reviewer would credit as a small contribution; four and five are stances. They are unvalidated outside the author's estate: every exercise of the gate, the cues and the pinned decisions happened in the author's workspaces under the author's agents, and the framework's own Principle 2 concedes that self-description "does not by itself prove universality or adherence in another harness" ([AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md)). And none of them is what the README leads with: the README leads with "keeping what's stored coherent", which is the least defensible claim in the list, and buries the gate, the cue and the pin in the fourth and later screens.

## The field is arriving where MarkdownLLM stands; that validates the direction, not the framework

The 2025–2026 convergence is real and it runs in MarkdownLLM's direction. Letta, which invented self-editing memory blocks, deprecated its entire server-side memory model on 16 March 2026 and rebuilt memory as "git-backed context repositories" in which "every change to memory is automatically versioned with informative commit messages" and "Each subagent gets an isolated worktree… merged back through git-based conflict resolution" ([Letta, 12 Feb 2026](https://www.letta.com/blog/context-repositories); [Letta, 16 Mar 2026](https://www.letta.com/blog/our-next-phase/)). Anthropic shipped a client-side file memory tool (`memory_20250818`) on every Claude 4+ model and, for Managed Agents, path-addressed memory stores in which "Every change to a memory creates an immutable memory version" with `content_sha256` preconditions against concurrent writes and a "Dreaming" pass that "writes a separate output memory store and leaves the input untouched" ([memory tool](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/agents-and-tools/tool-use/memory-tool); [memory stores](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/managed-agents/memory); [Dreams](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/managed-agents/dreams)). EverMind's EverOS states "Markdown files are the source of truth. SQLite and LanceDB indexes sync from them" ([EverMemOS](https://github.com/EverMind-AI/EverMemOS)). GitHub stores Copilot memories with code citations and re-verifies them before use ([GitHub blog](https://github.blog/2026-01-09-building-an-agentic-memory-system-for-github-copilot)). Kilo deprecated its Memory Bank for AGENTS.md, Windsurf's migration guidance points away from local memories to rules files in the repo, and Cursor's Memories page has disappeared from its docs index ([Kilo](https://kilo.ai/docs/advanced-usage/memory-bank); [Devin Docs](https://docs.devin.ai/desktop/cascade/memories); [Cursor llms.txt](https://cursor.com/docs/llms.txt)). On the research side, the Always-On Agents survey coded 435 works and found the literature "focuses more on accumulating and retrieving state than on governing, recovering, or relinquishing it"; the security survey proposes PROV, versioned snapshots, write logs and rollback primitives; VerificAgent freezes a human-verified memory as a "safety contract" ([Always-On](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30306); [security survey](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.16548); [VerificAgent](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.02539v2)). Even the benchmark collapse points the same way: a plain filesystem with grep scored 74.0% on LoCoMo against Mem0's 68.5%, and the 2026 frontier results (Mastra, Hindsight, Honcho, EverOS) are all "compress history into dated, curated prose that the model reads" rather than fact triples in a vector store ([Letta benchmark](https://www.letta.com/blog/benchmarking-ai-agent-memory); [Mastra](https://mastra.ai/research/observational-memory)).

What this means, stated without softening: the direction is validated by others arriving at it, and that is not the same as MarkdownLLM being validated. Three distinctions keep the two apart. First, the convergers brought their users with them and MarkdownLLM has none to bring — Letta's pivot carried 15k PyPI downloads a month, Copilot Memory is default-on for every Pro subscriber, Anthropic's memory tool is on every Claude 4 model; the framework's adoption column reads one author and a partner operator who lost 90 minutes to its vocabulary. Second, none of the convergers adopted the parts MarkdownLLM is actually distinct on: Letta's dreaming "does not ask you for approval", Anthropic's stores retain versions for 30 days and have no human commit step, Copilot verifies against code and cannot verify intent or decisions, and nobody ships a schema floor, a persisted reconciliation question, or a commit-pinned decision record. The convergence is on the substrate — files, git, verification at some boundary — not on the governance MarkdownLLM built on top of it. Third, convergence cuts the other way on moats: if the substrate is becoming the industry default, the framework's differentiation shrinks to the three narrow mechanisms, and vendors are already absorbing adjacent functions — Claude Code's `/doctor prompt-audit` now "looks for… files that contradict each other" across CLAUDE.md, AGENTS.md, rules and skills, which is a lint over exactly the surfaces the framework calls definition surfaces ([Claude Code memory](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/memory)). The framework's own decision record names the moat as "what a domain accumulates when it is run: pinned decisions, resolved conflicts, verified imports, insights that survived retrospectives — an artifact no vendor's memory produces and no vendor owns" ([things/decisions/harness-native-onramp-supersedes-desktop.md](../things/decisions/harness-native-onramp-supersedes-desktop.md)). That is the right moat to name. It is also one whose entire evidence lives in private repositories the public cannot see, which means the public claim rests on the author's word.

The honest reading of the convergence for the author is therefore encouraging and sobering at once. Encouraging: the bet on files, git, deterministic checks and a human in the loop is the bet the governance literature now recommends and three vendors now make. Sobering: the field is "recommending rather than testing" that position, so there is still no external measurement showing it works; and the framework's lead on the parts that remain distinct is, in the prosecution's words, "a lead of months, not of kind" — Karpathy's gist is six weeks younger than the framework's first commit, gbrain's hook and Basic Memory's schema validation arrived within the same year, and community karpathy-llm-wiki skills already split lint into "deterministic auto-fixes" and "heuristic reports" ([karpathy-llm-wiki skill](https://skillselion.com/skills/astro-han/karpathy-llm-wiki)).

## What would make the strong claims defensible

Every item below is concrete and testable, and most are cheap. Several are already named in the repository's own backlog and have been outranked by new arcs for four months — "every review since June 11 ranked this work first and none of it moved" ([things/plans/evidence-and-eval-backlog.md](../things/plans/evidence-and-eval-backlog.md)).

| Claim to defend | What would make it defensible | Pass condition |
|---|---|---|
| "Used by someone other than the author" | A second independent operator outside the author's estate — not an employee, client or partner — scaffolds a workspace from the public repository alone, with no help from the author, and reaches a first validated commit; the session is recorded, the operator writes the review, and the author flips `verified` on it | Time to first validated commit, number of vocabulary questions, and the operator's own verdict are published in `reviews/`; the operator is a human, not a model session |
| "Structure beats scale" (Principle 9) | A fixture whose bare arm scores well below ceiling (non-saturating); a bare arm with the answer key *isolated*, not withheld ("Withholding is not isolation"); pinned model IDs; at least five trials per cell; pre-registered assertions; two task shapes separated — bookkeeping (where AI4AI's 0.49 → 0.91 and strict-format gains predict structure wins) and open-ended semantic ruling (where SPP and Hu et al. predict stronger models exploit structure better) ([AI4AI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12307); [SPP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.05300v1); [Hu et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32459)) | Framework-small beats bare-large on at least one shape with a confidence interval that excludes zero; the result is stated per shape, never pooled |
| "Keeps what's stored coherent" (the README lead) | A longitudinal coherence eval — the half the manifesto admits is "designed but not yet measured": seed N definition changes and M implicit contradictions (STALE-style) across K sessions in a framework arm and a CLAUDE.md-plus-notes-folder arm; measure detection rate, time-to-detection and false-positive (not-inflection) rate | Detection rate and precision reported against the plain-folder baseline; if the framework does not beat the baseline on detection, the lead sentence is rewritten to the narrower claim above |
| "Works from 10 to 10,000 things" | A validate-time series against thing count: emit `validate` wall time in the session-start digest, collect it across the 15 workspaces (555, 422, 337, 240 … 12 things) and the root, publish the curve, and reconcile `AGENTS.md` with the guide's 1,000 | The curve is in the repo; `AGENTS.md` states the measured ceiling; `thing-lifecycle.md` is deployed or formally retired on that evidence rather than "when felt" |
| "Novel", "Key Innovations" | A glossary mapping the coined vocabulary to standard terms, in the README and the kernel: floor → pre-commit validator; walk → impact analysis; cue → suspect link / open reconciliation item; reckoning → triage with exit rules; seat → advisory lease; estate → multi-repo workspace; porch → cross-workspace handoff; thing → typed record; and a prior-art column beside each "innovation" | A reader can judge novelty from the README alone; "Key Innovations" is retitled "Design choices and their prior art" |
| "Why not CLAUDE.md plus a notes folder?" | The answer the corpus "could win and never makes", written as `limitations.md`: the when-not-to-use list (high-volume transactional data, multi-writer teams, sub-second latency, genuinely relational queries), a worked scenario where the plain folder fails and the floor catches it, and the named competitors (Mem0, Zep, Letta, Copilot Memory, Karpathy) with what each does on "is it still true" | The file exists, the comparison names names, and it is linked from the README's second paragraph |
| "Tier 0 is emitted, never economised" | A session-start route that lands the digest whole as pulled content rather than relying on the budgeted hook: a slash-command / skill (`/orient`) whose body "enters the conversation as a single message" on invocation, or an explicit `Read` of `kernel.md` whose integrity trailer confirms receipt; then run `session-start-hardening` Phase 5 on two harnesses and score what was actually read ([Claude Code skills](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/skills)) | Phase 5 boxes are ticked with recorded transcripts; the `[truncated]` marker does not appear in the delivered digest; the kernel is received whole on the Claude Code channel or the entry file stops claiming it is |
| "The cue carrier works" | Treat the inflection rate as the mechanism's precision: 38 of 173 cues (22%) were ruled inflections. Measure whether the exhaust is felt (validation time, orient-view noise, clone size — the retrospective's own three indicators), tighten the trigger so that not-inflection cues fall, and deploy the rolling window for answered cues | Precision rises above 50% or the trigger is narrowed; answered cues no longer count against the validation walk |
| "Independent review" | Pay the owed verification (`verified: true` on the 39-day-old external review), and commission the next review from a human outside the estate or label every `reviews/` file as model-authored in its frontmatter | No review carries "independent" without naming whether its author was a person |

Two of these cost nothing but an afternoon — the glossary and the limitations file — and would remove the two largest sources of inflated novelty in one stroke. One, the slash-command route for session start, addresses the single sharpest weakness this review found and is well within the framework's existing skill machinery. The eval and the second operator are the expensive ones, and they are the only two that can turn the framework's central claims from hypotheses into results.

## Conclusion

The question the author asked has an answer that is uncomfortable in a specific way: MarkdownLLM's architecture is more defensible than its prose, and its prose is what the public sees. The framework declines to automate what forty years of belief-revision theory says cannot be automated, puts a deterministic check where the vendors' own documentation now admits one is needed, and routes irreversible judgement to a human — and then describes all of that in a private vocabulary that makes a DOORS suspect link look like an invention and a GitOps controller look like a doorbell. The three mechanisms that are genuinely without found precedent are buried under seven "innovations" that are not, and the one sentence the README leads with is the one sentence the framework has never measured and that five other systems already address in their own ways.

What has changed in understanding is the shape of the risk. The risk is not that MarkdownLLM is wrong about the problem — Mem0's retreat to ADD-only, the 55% stale-detection ceiling, and the one-in-five re-verification rate say the problem is exactly as hard as the README claims. The risk is that the industry converges on the substrate (files, git, verification) with real users while the framework's distinct governance layer stays unexercised outside one estate, its own agents skip the contract it emits, and the channel it chose to emit through cuts the contract at 10,000 characters. A framework whose thesis is that instructed content is economised and only emitted content is read has, on its primary harness, become instructed content. The fixes are known, several are cheap, and the repository's own backlog has ranked them first since June.

## Addendum 2026-10-18 — the operator's five questions, and Hermes

*Added by the framework agent the day after the review, on the operator's request: "what is special, what is duplicated, what is superseded or outdated, what should we stop doing, what should we do more of" — with Nous Research's Hermes Agent added to the comparison set, which the review above had missed. Sources read 2026-10-18. The review's rulings stand; this section ranks them for action.*

**Hermes Agent, in one paragraph.** Nous Research's open-source, MIT-licensed, self-hosted agent — "the agent that grows with you" — at **254.7k GitHub stars**, the largest adoption of any system in this review by an order of magnitude ([repository](https://github.com/nousresearch/hermes-agent); [docs](https://hermes-agent.nousresearch.com/docs)). Its memory is two files the agent curates through an add/replace/remove tool: `MEMORY.md` (environment facts, conventions, lessons) and `USER.md` (who the user is), under a **2,200-character ceiling**, injected into the system prompt at the *next* session start — a frozen snapshot, so "I told it to remember and it forgot" is a documented expected failure, not a bug. The agent "nudges itself to persist knowledge" on an undocumented cadence. Procedural memory is `SKILL.md` files in the agentskills.io open standard with a fixed section order (When to Use, Procedure, Pitfalls, Verification), authored by the agent after complex tasks and patched by it during use, loaded in three tiers (a ~3k-token list always in context, full skill on demand, reference files below). Cross-session recall is FTS5 full-text search over past transcripts with LLM summarisation; the Honcho plugin adds user modelling. It runs unattended on cron and meets the user in Telegram, Discord, Slack, WhatsApp, Signal and email ([TrueFoundry explainer](https://www.truefoundry.com/blog/what-is-hermes-agent); [third-party docs](https://github.com/mudrii/hermes-agent-docs)). **Nothing in its documentation validates, versions, pins, reconciles or asks a human about a memory write**; approvals exist for dangerous shell commands only. The one independent measurement, PAST-Bench (26 scenarios, 204 episodes, seven models, four frameworks), finds "improvement is real but uneven across capabilities", that agents "with the same headline gain can differ markedly in whether that gain is supported by evidence" of the save-retrieve-update pathway, and that the largest gain from its Hermes+ interventions came on tasks "needing outdated state replaced" — the update step is where the baseline is weakest ([PAST-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04003)). Hermes is therefore not a competitor on this framework's axis; it is the purest instance of the thing the framework's thesis says will rot — maximal autonomy, maximal distribution, a scratchpad the size of this paragraph, and no boundary — and the only external number about it says the staleness step is its weak one. Two things it does better than this framework, both decisive for adoption: it meets the user where they already are, and its skills are agent-written and self-patched in an open format, which is `workflows-emerge-from-use` shipped without a floor.

**1. Special — narrow, real, unvalidated outside one estate.** The review's five survivors, in the order a reviewer would credit them: the pre-commit gate that refuses a definition-surface commit until the dependant walk is on record; the reconciliation question persisted as a file until a human verdict; decision records pinned to commit SHAs with a reverse index; the stated floor/agent/human allocation; and specifications validated by the floor they impose. Against the full set — Mem0, Zep, Letta, Claude Code, Copilot, Karpathy, Cline, Basic Memory, spec-kit/Kiro, Backstage, org-mode, and now Hermes — this remains the only system in which "is it still true" is never settled silently by a model: Hermes, Letta, Zep and Mem0-2025 let the model settle it, Copilot re-checks against code at read time, and Hermes's own benchmark names the update step as the weak one. A sixth, less comfortable special quality: the record's candour about its own defects, which no competitor matches and no outsider reads.

**2. Duplicated — most of the surface, under a private vocabulary.** Tiered loading is Agent Skills' and Hermes's three levels exactly; the `*.skill.md` layer is a pre-standard private format for what agentskills.io now standardises and Hermes writes autonomously; AGENTS.md discovery is the industry convention; markdown-plus-git is now Letta's, Karpathy's, Basic Memory's, EverMind's and Hermes's substrate; frontmatter validated at a boundary is Astro, gbrain, Basic Memory; `type: example` is few-shot; lenses are six hats; nested repos are a gitignored inner repo; git-log velocity is event sourcing; the standing watch is a GitOps poll (Hermes ships cron too); the seat is an advisory lease. Eight coined terms appear roughly 2,000 times across the root and no glossary exists; the 90-minute onboarding wall is that cost measured once.

**3. Superseded or outdated — seven items, all on the public surface.** (a) The README's lead sentence: superseded by Zep's bi-temporal edges, Copilot's read-time verification, Karpathy's lint, Letta's dreaming and Claude Code's `/doctor prompt-audit`; the defensible narrower sentence is drafted above. (b) "10 to 10,000 things": contradicted by the framework's own guide, a 555-thing largest corpus and a 27.5-second validate. (c) Tier-0 delivery through the SessionStart hook: superseded on 2026-10-17 by `/orient` (commit e6ae01d), with the hook's share being retuned. (d) "That onramp is being built" in the README while the plan is paused and the Desktop route frozen. (e) The managed Session Start block that binds four prompts at t=0 in every domain's `AGENTS.md`: the instruction-time ritual Kilo deprecated and v3.33 re-homed to `orient` — domains carry the old block until they refresh. (f) Enumerations restated where the tool owns the set: the kernel's delivery-token list lagged by one token for the length of yesterday's build, nine weeks after the same failure with `related`. (g) `thing-lifecycle.md`, deferred since May for a ceiling the corpus has passed — not outdated, overdue.

**4. Stop doing.** Stop titling the seven items "Key Innovations" and stop the private register on outward surfaces: ship the glossary (floor → pre-commit validator, walk → impact analysis, cue → suspect link, reckoning → triage with exit rules, seat → advisory lease, estate → multi-repo workspace, porch → cross-workspace handoff, thing → typed record). Stop writing a file per answered cue by default: 173 in five weeks, 78% ruled not-inflection, 37% of `things/`; yesterday's additive route raised five cues whose every line was "consistent" except the files the same commit edited — the gate's precision is 22% and the carrier should be tightened or the answered cues rolled into the window, not both left as they are. Stop opening framework-about-framework arcs ahead of the two measurements: every plan title concerns the framework, thirteen high-priority plans are stalled, and the backlog has ranked the eval and the second operator first since June without either moving — close or honestly pause two of the thirteen before starting anything new. Stop calling model-commissioned reviews independent; this file names its authorship, the other seventeen should. Stop restating tool-owned enumerations in prose and in the kernel — point at the authority. Stop the README's forward-looking lines until they are true.

**5. Do more of.** The two measurements that turn hypotheses into results: a discriminating eval (non-saturating fixture, isolated bare arm, pinned model IDs, two task shapes reported separately) and the longitudinal coherence eval the manifesto admits is unmeasured — designed around exactly the step PAST-Bench and the stale-constraints paper name as the weak one, seeded definition changes and implicit contradictions over K sessions, framework arm against a CLAUDE.md-plus-notes arm, detection rate, time-to-detection and false-positive rate, so that Hermes-style agents can be measured on the same fixture. A second human operator from the public repository alone, recorded. Publish the numbers only this framework has — cue precision, validate time against thing count, longitudinal detection rate — as a dated series, since an honest small number beats a saturated benchmark. Write `limitations.md` with the "why not CLAUDE.md plus a notes folder" answer, naming Mem0, Zep, Letta, Copilot, Karpathy and Hermes; rewrite the README lead to the narrower sentence; both cost an afternoon. Reframe strategically toward interoperation rather than a parallel substrate: the field has converged on files and git, so the framework's remaining distinction is the gate, the cue, the pin and the human residue — adopt agentskills.io `SKILL.md` for the skills layer, keep AGENTS.md, and position the floor as the governance layer over what Letta, Claude and Hermes already read and write, not as their replacement. Show the moat the direction record names — the accumulated verified domain record — on one corpus the public can inspect, because today all of it is private. Deploy the rolling window. Finish `session-start-hardening`: the `/orient` acceptance run and the hook retune, so that the floor is actually received on the primary harness before any further claim about it.
